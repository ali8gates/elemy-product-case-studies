## Availability capture

| Measure | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agent phone time to collect availability | 25 minutes per prospective family | None |
| Who collected it | An agent, by phone, for every family including those who never enrolled | The family, self service |
| Time entry model | Specific intervals | Day and window buckets |
| Weekly minimum | 15 hours, communicated quietly rather than as a counter | Same, with validation moved after submit |
| Clinical recommendation | Often 25 to 30 hours per week | Unchanged, and buckets narrowed the gap to it |
| Hours offered per family | Baseline | Increased after buckets |
| Follow up for insufficient availability | Routine, handled by phone | Reduced, handled by in-product coaching |
| Schedulability of collected hours | Baseline | Held. No significant obstacles scheduling therapy later in the funnel |

## Onboarding program

| Measure | Result |

## Timeline

| Period | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| Late 2021 | Joined. Ran availability research personally, with no research function in place. Insights, ideation workshop, and three concepts inside the first 60 days |
| End of 2021 | Pulled to other work. Research banked |
| Early 2022 | Engineering capacity opened. Shipped the simple version in under a week to get storage of family time working |
| Mid 2022 | Formed the bucket hypothesis, ran a concept test with families from earlier studies, confirmed both that they offer more time and that they accept wider schedules |
| Later 2022 | Operations reported insufficient availability follow ups. Bucket version prioritized and delivered through a designer I managed |
| End of 2022 | Admissions running with 20 fewer agents. No phone time for availability collection |

Total time on the availability initiative was about nine months of on and off work, which is what a real priority looks like at a company growing this fast.
